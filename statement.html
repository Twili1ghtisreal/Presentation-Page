Why I Won't Look Away From This

On NSFW and sexualized content of the Doki Doki Literature Club characters, and, by the same argument, of characters in games, anime and stories generally.

Where this comes from

I've put thousands of hours into Doki Doki Literature Club and everything that's grown around it: Monika After Story, the mods, the community projects. In that time I've come to genuinely love every character in this game. Not in some ironic, detached way. It's actual affection, the kind you'd have for people you've spent years getting to know. That's exactly why I can't stay quiet when I see what gets done to them online.

I know how this comes across to some people. I know the reflex response: "it's just fictional, it doesn't hurt anyone, calm down." I've heard it before and I'll hear it again. I'm writing this anyway, because I think the reflex response skips past the actual point instead of answering it.

The thought experiment

Here's the simplest way I can put what I feel. Think of someone you actually love (a partner, a close friend, someone real in your life, or even a fictional character you've grown attached to the way I have with these characters). Now imagine thousands, maybe millions, of strangers taking that person's image, their face, their personality, and using it however they personally want: sexually, degradingly, without their say in it at all.

That discomfort you'd feel imagining that is the same thing I feel every time I scroll past this content on TikTok, Reddit, wherever it's turned up this week. It's not about being easily offended. It's that these characters were written with real intention, real voice, real emotional weight, and I've spent real time caring about who they are. Watching that get flattened into something to be used doesn't sit right with me, and I don't think it should have to.

It's not just "they're not real"

The usual counter is that fictional characters can't be harmed, so nothing here actually matters. I'd push back on that a little differently than most people do. It's not really about whether Sayori or Natsuki can feel anything. Obviously they can't. It's about what the content says about how the people making and consuming it are choosing to treat a story that, for a lot of us, meant something real.

DDLC's characters weren't built to be sexualized. If anything, the game goes out of its way to make you sit with how young, how vulnerable, and how human these characters are supposed to feel. Taking that and reshaping it into fetish content isn't some neutral act happening in a vacuum. It's actively working against what the writing was doing in the first place. That's the part that bothers me more than the fictional-status debate ever could.

It's not only about this game

I want to be clear that none of this stops at DDLC. This game is just where it lands closest, because it's the one I've spent the most years inside, but the same thing turns up with other games, with anime, with manga, with any story where someone sat down and put real care into a character. If the writing gave them a voice, a history and a reason to care, then nothing above changes because the title on the cover does.

And I'm not drawing the line at "the characters I happen to like", either. Something gets popular, the people in it start getting treated like an inventory of parts, and whoever wrote them has no say in it. That's a pattern I've watched across fandoms long enough to know it never stays inside one of them. What bothers me isn't which game it is. It's the habit underneath, and the habit travels.

It turns up in cosplay too

This isn't only about edits and fan art. It shows up around DDLC cosplay on TikTok and other platforms too, and I think it's worth saying out loud. Plenty of cosplayers clearly love the characters and put real work into their costumes, and most of that has nothing to do with any of this.

What I mean is narrower. On some posts, the comments have nothing to do with the costume. There's nothing about accuracy, the character or the effort, just reactions to the person's body. I can't know what any individual cosplayer intends, and I'm not going to guess. But platforms reward that kind of engagement, and it shapes who shows up in the comments.

Then there's the far end of it: accounts that use a DDLC costume as the setup for adult content, with the character's name in the tags, the title or the bio so the right audience finds it. That isn't cosplay as a hobby. It's using the character as a hook to draw people toward sexual content. I'm not naming anyone, and I'm not talking about cosplayers who just love the characters, or who get unwanted comments on a normal post. The line I'm drawing is between someone dressing up as a character they love and someone using that character's name to pull in an audience for sexual content.

It's the same issue as everything above, on a different platform. These characters were written with real vulnerability and depth, and when they become a hook for that kind of attention, something of what made the game worth caring about gets lost.

It's on every platform

None of this lives in one corner of the internet. I see it on X, on Reddit, in Discord servers, on TikTok, and even on YouTube, where it's wrapped in thumbnails and titles that are built to be clicked. Different platform, same pattern: the lewd edit or the suggestive post goes up, it gets the engagement, and it gets treated as just another piece of the fandom.

There's a quieter version of it too. Plenty of posts don't contain anything explicit themselves; they point toward it. A "check out this artist" post, a repost with the artist tagged, a link in a bio, a recommendation thread. A big one is sharing art from X: the piece itself looks harmless, but the account it comes from is tied to lewd or NSFW content, and the post sends people straight to it. Some of those artists draw wholesome work, and I have no problem with that. But a lot of them are best known for NSFW art of these characters, and sharing their work or sending people their way does the same job as posting the content directly. It's how the stuff reaches people who never went looking for it.

And it isn't just small accounts. I've seen it from bigger content creators too, people with anywhere from 10k to 300k subscribers, who post or feature this kind of art, or art from accounts connected to it, in front of audiences that size. That matters more than a random post does. A creator that big has a community that trusts them and often a lot of younger viewers, so when they put it on screen, it tells all of those people it's normal and shows them where to find more. I'd expect more care from someone with that much reach, not less.

I'm not naming anyone here, and this isn't about picking on individual artists. It's about the way it spreads: one platform's "safe" post becomes another platform's way in. When it's everywhere, it stops reading as a few people's taste and starts reading as the default, and that's exactly how it ends up in front of the next newcomer who just wanted to talk about the game.

What happened when I said something

This stopped being hypothetical for me recently. In one of the biggest DDLC spaces online (a server plenty of people here would recognise), suggestive and lewd content gets posted and treated as nothing worth remarking on. So I said what I actually thought: that these characters get sexualized, reduced to fetishes, and stripped of the emotional weight their story gave them. I wasn't expecting everyone to agree. I was expecting a conversation.

What I got instead was two kinds of shutdown. From the members, dismissal: "it's just the internet", "it's not that deep", "just don't look at it". From the staff, something worse: a moderator who never once addressed the content that started it, but was quick to warn me and threaten me with a ban for speaking about it. They told me to go and read the rules of the server they moderate. They weren't following them either.

That's the part I keep coming back to. The rulebook existed. It just wasn't the thing being enforced. What was being enforced was the quiet: don't make the space feel uncomfortable for the people who want to use it that way.

The Monika After Story community

Monika After Story is the part of this fandom I've given the most time to, which is why this one is hard to write. The main MAS Discord server has around 22K members ("around" because the number moves, and that's roughly what it was when I wrote this). In my experience, a lot of the people in that community are rude, and it gets worse the moment this subject comes up. You don't get a conversation. You get brushed off, snapped at, or treated as the problem for noticing. I've written about it in more detail in a post on Reddit.

There's also a server called NSFW MAS Server, with roughly 5k members. I don't know whether it's officially affiliated with the main MAS server, and I'm not claiming it is. I'm not going to link it either. But it's built entirely around the same project and the same character, and from the outside it reads as part of the same fandom. A community that size, organised around sexual content of Monika, tells every newcomer who finds it that this is just one more normal corner of MAS.

I'm naming servers here, not people. This isn't about any one member. It's that a community this big, around a project this wholesome in its origins, can end up being known for how it treats the people who ask for a bit of care.

Why a lot of people won't understand this

Most of the people I'm describing will read this and feel nothing. Some will feel attacked. I don't think that's stubbornness. Spend enough time around this content and it stops being something you chose. It just becomes normal. It's what you open the app to. It's in the group chat. It's the joke everyone in the server already knows. After a while your brain files it under "apparently fine".

So the people defending it aren't lying when they say they don't see a problem. They really don't. Their normal has moved, and from where they're standing nothing looks wrong about it. That's less like having a taste for something and more like being hooked on it.

That's the hard part. When something already feels normal, "this isn't okay" doesn't land as an opinion you can argue with. It lands as an accusation. And the reflex is to make it go away. I'm not a doctor and I'm not diagnosing anyone from a distance. I just think it's worth saying plainly: a big part of why this is so hard to explain is that the people who most need it explained have been around it long enough that it can't bother them anymore.

Why I hold the line on this

I'm not interested in policing what strangers do in corners of the internet I'll never see. But in spaces I actually have a say over (communities I help build, servers I run), I'm not going to pretend this is a matter of taste I should stay neutral on. A fandom that lets this kind of content sit unchallenged ends up normalizing it for the next wave of newcomers, and I'd rather be the person saying something than the person who let it slide. Having actually done that now, in a space that big, in front of that many people, I can tell you it costs more than it looks like it should. I'd do it again.

If you disagree, that's fine. You're allowed to. I'm not writing this expecting to change every mind. But I know I'm not the only person who loves this game and feels sick seeing what's done to its characters, and I wanted that put into words somewhere more permanent than a comment that'll get buried by tomorrow.

Written out of genuine love for this game and its characters, not out of a wish to control anyone else's fandom.
